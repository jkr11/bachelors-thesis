Tensor Networks \cite{cuiperHouchesLectureNotes2026} allow the efficient
numerical representations and computation on quantum states for large $N$. This
includes variational methods and efficient simulation of quantum circuits
\cite{orusAdvancesTensorNetwork2014,biamonteLecturesQuantumTensor2020}.
Symmetric Tensor Networks \cite{singhTensorNetworkDecompositions2010a} yield a
speedup over traditional methods for a subclass of tensor networks that are
globally symmetric. The study of symmetric tensors is largely related to the
structure of the \emph{categories of representations} $\Rep(G)$ for some
symmetry group $G$ such as $\SU{2}$. These representation categories are
foundational to the theory behind \emph{fusion tensor categories}

\paragraph{Overview} This is an overview of the individual chapters and their contents.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \Cref{sect:background} gives an introduction to tensors and tensor
    networks. We provide the folklore definition of a tensor as a multilinear
    arrays of complex numbers, and generalize this to a definition over abstract
    vector spaces. We collect all the necessary operations of a tensor into the
    einsum notation. We also define the theory of tensor networks, and we give
    the most common structures and algorithms.
    \item \Cref{sect:symmetricTensorNetworks} first introduces the concept of a
    symmetry, and we also give a short review of representation theory. We show
    how the XXZ-hamiltonian factors into a block-sparse matrix under its
    symmetry groups $\U{1},\operatorname{O}(2)$ and $\SU{2}$. We then begin
    defining symmetric tensors, so tensors that are invariant under the global
    application of a symmetry $g \in G$. We show that we can achieve a speedup
    on tensors of any shape, however the actual algorithms by which these sparse
    block tensors are realized are given in \Cref{sect:Categories}\todo{index
    the subsection} so we do not produce redundant texts.
    \item \Cref{sec:anyonsIntro} is an introduction to the theory of
    \emph{anyons}. We introduce them as pointlike defects in a two-dimensional
    medium. We then show that their time-evolutions are governed by the
    structure of the \emph{braid-group}, and its higher dimensional
    representations. We sketch a derivation of their fusion relations from this
    exchange structure. 
    \item \Cref{sect:Categories} forms the backbone of this thesis. First, we
    give an introduction to category theory from the ground up. Then, we begin a
    step-by-step derivation of the theory of \emph{modular tensor categories},
    motivated by our knowledge about representations and the physical aspects of
    the anyons. Each step carries along a visual interpretation in terms of
    standard vectors spaces, in terms of representations and in terms of anyon
    diagrammatics. Then, in \Cref{DecompositionOfSemisimpleCategories} we prove
    the sparse-block decomposition of a tensor for arbitrary fusion categories.
    In \Cref{...}, we derive how to implement these categorical tensors on the
    basis of this decomposition. We give a formal definition of a
    \emph{fusion-tree} and how to represent this in a program. As above, general
    einsums will be implemented.
    \item \Cref{sect:Anyons} should form the last chapter \todo{Does it?}. Here
    we explore some of the more elementary models on anyon systems in one and
    two-dimensions. Then we introduce the theory of \emph{topological quantum
    computation}, and we show how to simulate this by our categorical tensor
    networks.
\end{itemize}
